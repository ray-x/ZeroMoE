Explain how a modern mixture-of-experts language model serves inference when the model is much larger than the available GPU memory. Cover the roles of dense attention weights, routed expert weights, host memory, and a small device cache. Discuss why the order in which experts are requested matters, how a scheduler can overlap host-to-device transfers with computation, and what trade-offs arise between cache capacity, latency, and memory bandwidth. Then describe how a full-graph execution plan differs from a layer-at-a-time plan, including the costs of building the graph, uploading weights, and reusing state between the prompt and decode phases. Finally, summarize the measurements one should report to make a throughput claim credible, such as first-token latency, graph construction time, steady-state decode rate, cache hit rate, and peak host and device memory. Use concrete examples where helpful, but do not assume any particular hardware.
